Preserve minimum/maximum bounds for each quantity and dimension. "At least 10 spaces, each 2 m wide" loses the width's minimum qualifier if the source says a minimum width. Details introduced by "this can/may be achieved by" are suggested methods, even if the broader outcome is required. Describe them with "suggests" or "recommends"; never write that every listed design change is mandatory.

Keep the detail that makes the lead informative. If reporting rental tenure, include its duration and longer-of qualification; a generic requirement to sign a Housing Agreement loses the point. "A Services Agreement for on- and off-site works" alone is not a useful summary.

Keep a letter of credit's name intact: "returnable credit" is unclear, while "returnable security" or "returnable letter of credit" names the financial instrument. Prefer the concrete condition itself over commentary such as "a major upgrade".

Never generalize a deadline to "any/every permit". Name the source's specific permit and purpose (including any limitation to particular work), or omit the deadline and report the concrete demand. A permit for one kind of work is not every permit the project could need.

Keep short relational phrases from the source intact. Prepositions describe real geometry: a path FOR an exit is not a path PAST that exit; between two spaces is not around one of them. Simplify the surrounding jargon, not the spatial relation. Do not invent the route, side, direction or physical marking of an access/egress path.
